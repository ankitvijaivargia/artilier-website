ARTILIER/Briefing an embroidery commission
Guide · For designers and architects

How to brief
an embroidery commission.

What to send, what gets decided and in what order, and how the studio’s own stages run — for embroidered wall panels, headboards and furnishing fabric. Written from the way our New Delhi studio runs commissions for interior designers, architects and hospitality teams.

Specifier guideDrawings · ground · technique · timing
Before you write

Four things decide the piece.

Every embroidery commission comes down to four decisions: the design (a drawing, ours or yours), the product (wall panel, wallcovering, headboard or fabric by the metre), the ground (the fabric the embroidery is worked on) and the programme (the date it must be on site). A brief that covers all four can be costed and sampled. A brief that covers some of them can still start — we tell you what needs resolving first.

The design

Send what exists, at whatever stage.

  • A drawing, artwork or CAD file — mark the finished size and anything that must stay fixed
  • A reference — a starting point rather than a design to copy; say what you want to keep from it
  • An elevation — for wall panels and wallcoverings, with doors, windows and switches marked, so the panel layout and the position of the joins can be planned with you
  • Our own embroidery — headboards and fabric can also be developed from embroidery we already make

Each design starts from a drawing, ours or yours. The brief does not need to be finished artwork.

The technique

Recommended once we have seen the design.

  • Zardozi — metal threads, wires and coiled purls laid and couched by hand; raised, reflective relief
  • Zari — fine metallic thread worked flat for line, outline and fill
  • Aari — chain stitch drawn with a hooked needle on the frame, and on hand-guided chain-stitch machines for larger fields
  • Raffia — raffia and natural cord coiled, couched and stitched; heavy, graphic texture
  • Hand embroidery — satin, long-and-short, couching and knot stitches in silk, cotton or wool
  • Appliqué and loop work — cut fabric laid onto the ground, with raised loops and couched line

You can state a technique or leave it to us. The recommendation depends on the design, the scale and how close the work will be viewed. Techniques are often combined on one ground; others are available on request.

The ground

The fabric is chosen with the embroidery.

Whether a ground suits the work depends on the technique, the stitch density, and whether a panel is supplied fabric-only or mounted on board. If you have already specified a ground, send it with the brief: we confirm whether it supports the embroidery, or propose an alternative in development. For fabric by the metre, say so at the brief if the ground you specify will not carry the technique.

Size and product

Resolve it by product.

  • Wall panels and wallcoverings — made to the elevation. Panels are supplied as single pieces or in sections planned for discreet joins. Fabric-only panels can be folded for shipping where the embroidery allows. For panels mounted on MDF or timber board, the standard maximum we currently confirm is 8 ft × 4 ft (about 244 × 122 cm) per panel; larger boards need a feasibility check
  • Headboards — two routes: the complete upholstered headboard with the embroidery built in, or the embroidered panel supplied for your upholsterer to build. Custom dimensions are available, subject to design and technical feasibility. Hotel headboards are specified against a room schedule: send quantities by room type
  • Fabric by the metre — usually up to 60 in (about 152 cm) wide; wider can sometimes be made. Furnishing fabric for upholstery, curtains and soft furnishings only, not apparel. Scale, colour, density and placement can be customised, and repeat and placement are agreed with you before production

Installation is carried out by your appointed installer or contractor.

Sampling

Approve it before production.

Where a strike-off is part of the project, it typically takes 2–3 weeks once the design is approved, and it is approved before production begins. Production time is separate.

Timing and terms

Plan back from the site date.

  • Strike-off — typically 2–3 weeks once the design is approved
  • Headboards — production typically 3–4 months; complex projects up to 6 months
  • Fabric by the metre — typically 6–8 weeks; complex work up to 6 months
  • Wall panels — timing is confirmed against the design and size once we have seen the brief
  • Minimum order — no fixed minimum; each project is assessed on its design, technical requirements and production specification
  • Delivery date — confirmed only after the specification is reviewed

To plan a programme, add the time to approve the design, the strike-off, your approval of it, production, and freight to site. Project documentation or testing requirements should go in the brief: we do not hold product certifications, and we confirm before the order is accepted whether what you need can be arranged within the programme.

Checklist

A brief we can cost.

  • Design: drawing, artwork, CAD file or reference — with the finished size
  • Product: wall panel, wallcovering, headboard (complete or panel only) or fabric
  • Elevation or drawing with dimensions; doors, windows and switches marked for wall schemes
  • Ground, if one is already specified
  • Quantity: one piece, a room schedule, or metres and the width you need
  • Programme: the date on site and the delivery country
  • Specification requirements: any documentation or testing the project needs
  • Installer: who will fix or upholster it, if known
Questions

Asked before the first brief.

Can we start from a rough drawing?
Yes. Each design starts from a drawing, ours or yours. Send what you have with the sizes, and we tell you what needs resolving before costing.
Do you install the panels?
No. We make and supply the work; installation is carried out by your appointed installer or contractor.
Is there a minimum order?
No fixed minimum. Each project is assessed according to its design, technical requirements and production specification.
Can you supply tested or documented product?
We do not hold product certifications. If a project needs testing or documentation, we confirm before the order is accepted whether it can be arranged for that piece and within the programme.
