Guide · For designers and architects

How to brief
a bespoke rug.

What to send, what gets decided and in what order, and how long the studio’s own stages take — written from the way our New Delhi studio runs commissions for interior designers, architects and hospitality teams. Use it as a checklist before you write to any rug maker, including us.

Before you write

Four things decide a rug.

Every bespoke rug comes down to four decisions: the design (what it shows and how much detail it carries), the size and shape (what the room and the furniture plan need), the application (how much traffic the room takes, and what sits on the rug) and the programme (the date the rug must be on site). A brief that covers all four can be costed and sampled. A brief that covers only some of them can still start — we will tell you what needs resolving before anything can be costed properly.

The design

Send what exists, at whatever stage.

  • A painting or artwork — in any medium. Tell us the size of the original; it helps judge how the detail will scale to the finished rug
  • A drawing or CAD file — vector or scanned. Mark the intended finished size and anything that must stay fixed, such as a border width or a motif that must align with a room feature
  • A reference image — a starting point rather than a design to copy; say what you want to keep from it
  • A colour scheme — colours are matched by hand and approved in the hand, so send the references the scheme already uses
  • A collection design — any design in the collection can be re-scaled, re-proportioned and re-coloured to your scheme

A rough idea and a floor plan are enough to begin. Development — construction, yarn, colour separation and scale — is worked out with you, so the brief does not need to be finished artwork.

The size

Resolve it from the plan.

Rugs are made to order, so the size is set by the room rather than by a standard range. What is achievable still depends on the design and the construction, so very large or unusually shaped pieces are confirmed with you before sampling. The most useful thing you can send is the floor plan with the furniture layout, so proportions can be resolved before sampling. On the plan, mark:

  • The rug outline you intend — with dimensions in metres, centimetres or feet and inches, whichever your drawings use
  • Furniture positions — which pieces sit on the rug and which sit off it
  • Doors, thresholds and fixed features — anything the rug edge has to clear or align with
  • Several rugs? — send a rug schedule: room, quantity and size for each

For very large rooms, any size limit for the chosen construction is confirmed before sampling.

The construction

A technical decision, not a style one.

  • Hand-knotted — knot by knot on the loom: suited to fine detail and painterly artwork, and the longest to make. Hand-knotted designs
  • Hand-tufted — carving, mixed pile heights and loop-and-cut surfaces, with a shorter lead time than hand-knotting. Hand-tufted designs
  • Flatweave — low, graphic and lighter: for geometric work, layering and rooms where a pile would sit too high. Flatweave designs

You can state a preference or leave it to our recommendation. Either way, the construction is recommended from the artwork, the detail it carries, the traffic the room will take and the budget — and proved in sampling before production.

The yarn

Specified against the room.

The yarns we work with are New Zealand wool, hand-spun wool, wool-silk blends, bamboo silk, cotton (for warps and flatweaves), allo and hemp, and jute — with blends developed to the design where no single yarn does the job. Yarn, spin and dye lot are approved in the hand against your scheme before a loom is set.

So tell us where each rug sits and what it will take — a bedroom, a dining room under chairs, a hotel lobby. Yarn is specified against the construction and the traffic, and where a material is unsuitable for the room we say so. If the project sets performance, cleaning or documentation requirements, put them in the brief: we confirm what can be met, and what can be documented, before costing. More on rug materials.

Sampling

Approve it in the hand before the loom.

  • Yarn samples — approved in the hand against your scheme
  • Colour trials — against your scheme
  • A woven strike-off — ready 2–3 weeks after the design or sketch is approved. Its size depends on the design, and its cost is credited against the order
  • Then production — the full rug is set on the loom only once the strike-off is approved, and every finished rug is checked against the approved strike-off before dispatch

On projects with a mock-up room, give us the mock-up date and the rug types it needs; sampling is scheduled back from it.

Timing and terms

Plan back from the site date.

  • Strike-off — 2–3 weeks after the design or sketch is approved
  • Production — usually 6–12 weeks from strike-off approval, up to six months in some cases; hand-knotting takes longest, hand-tufting is shorter
  • Minimum order — none; a single rug is welcome
  • Quotes — in USD or EUR, usually ex-works; FOB or DDP quoted separately on request
  • Shipping — rugs usually travel rolled; payment terms are set out in the quote

To plan a programme, add together the time to approve the design, 2–3 weeks for the strike-off, your approval of it, production, and freight to site. We confirm the timeline for each project and tell you about changes as they arise. Orders are contracted, invoiced and exported through one entity, Art Avenue Pvt. Ltd., of which Artilier is the trade studio.

Checklist

A brief we can cost.

  • Design: artwork, CAD file, reference or collection design — with the size of the original
  • Size and shape: outline on the floor plan, units stated, furniture marked
  • Construction: a preference, or “recommend one”
  • Application: room type and traffic, plus any performance or documentation requirements
  • Colour: the scheme’s references
  • Programme: the date the rug must be on site, any mock-up date, delivery city
  • Quantity: one rug, or a schedule of several
Questions

Asked before the first brief.

Can we start from a rough sketch?
Yes. A rough idea and a floor plan are enough to begin; construction, yarn, colour separation and scale are developed with you, and we tell you what needs resolving before costing.
Do you work with private clients?
No. Artilier works to the trade only — interior designers, architects, hospitality teams and design practices. See trade.
Can one rug be adapted from the collection rather than developed from scratch?
Yes. Collection designs can be re-scaled, re-proportioned and re-coloured to your scheme, and made to the size the room needs within what the design and construction allow.
What happens if the strike-off is not right?
The full rug is not set on the loom until the strike-off is approved. If it is not right, tell us what needs to change; what a revision involves is agreed with you before anything further is made.
