How they are made

Three constructions.
One specialism.

Which construction a design wants is a technical decision, not a style one. We make the recommendation from the artwork, the detail it carries, the traffic it will take and the budget — and prove it in sampling before production.

01Hand-knotted

Knot by knot on the loom

Traditional hand-knotting for high-detail, high-density rugs. The finest definition, the longest lead time, and the construction that carries painterly artwork best.

02Hand-tufted

Tufted to the drawing

A more flexible construction for certain designs, textures and project requirements — carving, mixed pile heights and loop-and-cut surfaces, at a shorter lead time.

03Flatweave

Low profile, woven flat

Low-profile woven rugs suited to specific applications and design requirements — lighter, reversible in some constructions, and a different surface underfoot.

Made to order, sized to the project. Every rug is woven to the size, shape and colourway the project needs, within what the design and construction allow — the pieces shown across this site are references, not stock.

Inside the making

How each construction
is actually made.

Photographs and film from the workshops we weave with — the loom, the yarn, the hand, the finishing. Not a catalogue: this is the process behind hand-knotting, hand-tufting and flatweaving, so you can see how a rug of each kind comes into being. The designs themselves sit on the collection pages.

Process 01

Hand-knotting

Knot by knot on the loom

The loom is dressed, the design mapped to the warp, then knotted row by row — months of work on a large piece — before clipping, washing and stretching.

Hand-knotted designs →
Process 02

Hand-tufting

Drawn onto the frame, tufted by hand

The design is traced onto the backing cloth, tufted yarn by yarn, then latexed, backed, sheared and carved to bring the drawing out of the pile.

Hand-tufted designs →
Process 03

Flatweaving

Woven flat, no pile

Warp dressed on the loom and weft beaten in by hand, so the structure itself carries the design — then edges bound and the rug washed and finished.

Rug materials

Material selection is
part of the design.

Yarn decides how a rug reads long before the colour does — how light moves across the pile, how the surface wears, how sharply a detail holds. We select and blend for the design and the application, not from a standard menu.

Yarn, spin and dye lot are approved in the hand against your scheme before a loom is set. Where a material is unsuitable for the traffic a room will take, we say so.

Process

From artwork
to floor.

Six stages, adapted to the piece. Some commissions move quickly; others need more development. We keep the process transparent, with approvals and progress updates at the stages that matter.

01UnderstandArtwork, dimensions, colour scheme and application. We read it properly, then come back with our thoughts, our questions and what still needs resolving.
02DevelopConstruction, yarn, knot count, pile height, colour separation and scale worked through with the weaving workshop. Where there is enough information, an initial cost range follows.
03SampleYarn samples, colour trials and a woven strike-off — approved in the hand before full production begins.
04MakeKnotting, tufting or flatweaving to your dimensions, in the workshop suited to the work. Progress photography at agreed points.
05CheckQuality control against the specification and approved sample — dimensions, pile, colour, binding, backing and finishing, reviewed in Delhi.
06DeliverRolling, wrapping, export documentation and freight coordinated through our logistics partners. Installation is carried out by your appointed contractor; we remain available to answer questions until the piece is in place.
