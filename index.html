04 — Rug construction

Three constructions.
One specialism.

Which construction a design wants is a technical decision, not a style one. We make the recommendation from the artwork, the detail it carries, the traffic it will take and the budget — and prove it in sampling before production.

01Hand-knotted

Knot by knot on the loom

Traditional hand-knotting for high-detail, high-density rugs. The finest definition, the longest lead time, and the construction that carries painterly artwork best.

02Hand-tufted

Tufted to the drawing

A more flexible construction for certain designs, textures and project requirements — carving, mixed pile heights and loop-and-cut surfaces, at a shorter lead time.

03Flatweave

Low profile, woven flat

Low-profile woven rugs suited to specific applications and design requirements — lighter, reversible in some constructions, and a different surface underfoot.

Any design, any dimension. Every rug is woven to the size, shape and colourway of the project — the pieces shown across this site are references, not stock.

Photographs and film from the workshops we weave with — the loom, the yarn, the hand, the finishing. Not a catalogue: this is the process behind hand-knotting, hand-tufting and flatweaving, so you can see how a rug of each kind comes into being. The designs themselves sit on the collection pages.

Process 01

Hand-knotting

Knot by knot on the loom

The loom is dressed, the design mapped to the warp, then knotted row by row — months of work on a large piece — before clipping, washing and stretching.

Process 02

Hand-tufting

Drawn onto the frame, tufted by hand

The design is traced onto the backing cloth, tufted yarn by yarn, then latexed, backed, sheared and carved to bring the drawing out of the pile.

Process 03

Flatweaving

Woven flat, no pile

Warp dressed on the loom and weft beaten in by hand, so the structure itself carries the design — then edges bound and the rug washed and finished.

The finished rug matters. So does how it came into being — the loom, the knot, the shears, the hand that decides when a pile is right.

09 — Philosophy

Precision,
with character.

A hand-made rug is made by people, not machines. We work carefully to the agreed artwork, dimensions, materials and finish — and at the same time, natural yarn and human hands leave their own trace. Small differences in weave, pile and colour are part of what makes a hand-made rug individual.

We don't try to remove that character. We try to make sure it is intentional and considered.

What is checked

  • Dimensions against the approved artwork
  • Materials approved before production
  • Finishes reviewed against the sample
  • Samples or strike-offs before production
  • Workmanship inspected before dispatch

What is character

  • Natural variation in yarn and spin
  • Subtle differences in hand-finishing
  • Variation across a hand-woven pile
  • Slight differences in dye lot and abrash
  • Individual marks left by the making

Handmade does not mean careless. A defect is a defect — it is not dismissed as character.

10 — Process

From artwork
to floor.

Six stages, adapted to the rug. Some commissions move quickly; others need more development. We keep the process transparent, with approvals and progress updates at the stages that matter.

01UnderstandArtwork, dimensions, colour scheme and application. We read it properly, then come back with our thoughts, our questions and what still needs resolving.
02DevelopConstruction, yarn, knot count, pile height, colour separation and scale worked through with the weaving workshop. Where there is enough information, an initial cost range follows.
03SampleYarn samples, colour trials and a woven strike-off — approved in the hand before the full rug is set on the loom.
04MakeWeaving, tufting or flatweaving to your dimensions, in the workshop suited to the construction. Progress photography at agreed points.
05CheckQuality control against the specification and approved sample — dimensions, pile, colour, binding, backing and finishing, reviewed in Delhi.
06DeliverRolling, wrapping, export documentation and freight coordinated through our logistics partners — and we stay available through installation.

Costing and lead times become more precise as the artwork, construction, materials and sampling requirements are understood. Hand-made production can vary with construction, yarn availability and loom capacity — we communicate changes as they arise.
